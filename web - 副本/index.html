Section 01 · The Clinical Stakes

One image, two diagnoses, opposite next steps.

Six months after radiotherapy for a high-grade glioma, follow-up MRI almost always shows a new contrast-enhancing lesion. The two pathologies it could be look nearly identical — yet demand opposite clinical actions.

01 · Patient presents
A 58-year-old, 9 months post-RT for grade IV glioma. Follow-up MRI shows a new T1ce-enhancing lesion at the resection margin.
02 · Radiologist asks
Is it the tumour coming back — or the radiation that killed it? Standard MRI can't tell. Definitive diagnosis today requires repeat craniotomy and histopathology.
03 · Two paths

if recurrence

Immediate second-line therapy

Bevacizumab, re-resection, or salvage radio-chemotherapy. Delay costs survival weeks.

→ A missed recurrence is a treatment window lost.

if necrosis

Conservative management

Steroids, anti-inflammatories, observation. Treating it as recurrence means an unnecessary craniotomy.

→ A missed necrosis is a surgery the patient did not need.

Section 03 · Missing-Modality Synthesis · The Innovation

Where the data ends, the prior begins.

Instead of zero-filling a missing FLAIR channel — which the model would gate out — we synthesise it from a domain-informed linear recipe over the z-scored modalities. The model sees a real-looking channel and routes through its FLAIR-aware pathway as if nothing was missing.
